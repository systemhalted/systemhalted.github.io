At 8:50 in the morning, my mobile phone rang.

I was fast asleep, lost in that sweet, undisturbed morning slumber, so it took several rings before my brain even registered the sound. Groping around the bed with one eye open, I finally picked it up to hear Gajendra on the line.

Now, this was completely unexpected. Just yesterday, he was excitedly packing his bags and telling me about his weekend trip to Ratnagiri with a group of friends—beaches, Konkan breeze, and a couple of days away from the city.

"Bhai, programme cancel ho gaya," he announced casually.

The trip was off. And before I could even process his bad luck or offer any sympathy, he dropped his alternative:

"Uth ja, office pahunch." (Wake up and head to the office.)

Office? On a Saturday when we were supposed to take it easy? But there was a supposedly noble mission behind it: we had IT certifications to prepare for. So I snapped into action. I rushed through my morning chores in under thirty minutes and hurried out the door.

It was a crisp Pune morning. It took a solid 25-minute brisk walk from my room to reach Sanghavi Phata. Once there, I stood by the roadside, squinting into the traffic, hoping to catch the Infosys company bus. But patience was in short supply, and the company bus clearly had its own schedule. So I did what every Pune commuter does when time is ticking: I hopped onto a shared six-seater auto. Rattling along with the diesel engine roaring and the morning wind in my face, I finally reached the Hinjewadi campus.

Gajendra and Pallav arrived shortly after.

With great determination, the three of us pulled up chairs, sat down at our desks, and cracked open the certification material. We were going to be serious professionals today. We were going to conquer the syllabus.

That heroic resolve lasted all of ten minutes.

None of us could read even two sentences without our minds wandering off. Technical jargon on an unplanned morning after a cancelled beach holiday is simply too much to ask of human nature.

Gajendra looked up, Pallav smiled, and without another word, we quietly minimized the study guides. Someone fired up the music player. The speakers filled the room with songs, and we sank back into those plush revolving office chairs—talking, laughing, and gently drifting away into our own daydreams.

So why am I writing this?

Honestly, there is no moral here. No career milestone. No grand philosophy.

/Bas aise hi likh raha hoon./ Just writing for the sake of writing.

Because sometimes, the days where absolutely nothing goes according to plan—and nothing productive gets accomplished—are the quietest, best days you remember.

(And if you're still reading this... well, I did warn you in the title!)
