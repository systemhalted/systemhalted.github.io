For many of my friends who may not be familiar, Diwali is one of the most important and joyful celebrations in the Hindu tradition, but it's also observed by Jains, Sikhs, and Buddhists. Often called the "Festival of Lights," Diwali symbolizes the triumph of light over darkness, good over evil, and knowledge over ignorance. Think of it as a time similar to Christmas or Thanksgiving - filled with warmth, family, and festivities.

The story behind Diwali is rooted in Hindu mythology. It celebrates the return of Lord Rama to his kingdom after 14 years of exile, during which he fought and defeated the demon king Ravana. His homecoming was welcomed with thousands of oil lamps (called diyas), which is why lighting up homes and streets plays such a key role in the festival. It's a beautiful sight to see homes, temples, and public spaces glowing with light, symbolizing hope and the power of goodness.

But Diwali isn't just about lights. It's a five-day celebration[fn:1] where people clean and decorate their homes, wear new clothes, share sweets, and exchange gifts. Fireworks light up the sky in the evening as families come together to celebrate the joy and victory that Diwali represents. On a personal level, it's a time to reflect on the year gone by, give thanks, and pray for prosperity and peace in the year ahead.

Diwali is also about community and spreading joy. The spirit of generosity and sharing is at its heart. Families come together, neighbors visit each other, and many Hindus engage in charitable activities during the festival. It's about bringing light not just into our own lives but into the lives of others.

Whether you celebrate Diwali or not, the messages behind it are universal - hope, renewal, kindness, and the belief that good will always overcome evil. If you see friends posting about it or lighting candles, feel free to join in the spirit and wish them a Happy Diwali.

* Footnotes

[fn:1] Here are the 5 days with their hindi name and rough English translation:

1. Dhanteras (Day 1 - Day of Fortune) – Marks the beginning of Diwali and is dedicated to wealth and prosperity. People buy gold, silver, or utensils as a sign of good luck.
2. Naraka Chaturdashi (Chhoti Diwali) (Day 2 - Day of Knowledge) – This day celebrates the victory of Lord Krishna over the demon Narakasura, symbolizing the triumph of good over evil. It is also known as Chhoti (small) Diwali.
3. Lakshmi Puja (Day 3 - Diwali, which this year is on 31st Oct and 1st Nov) – The main day of Diwali, when homes are illuminated with lamps, and Goddess Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth, is worshiped for prosperity and happiness.
4. Govardhan Puja (Annakut) (Day 4 - Day of Offering and also new financial year) – Commemorates Lord Krishna’s lifting of Mount Govardhan to protect the villagers from torrential rains. People prepare large amounts of food and offer it to God.
5. Bhai Dooj (Day 5 - Day of Love between Siblings) – A day to celebrate the bond between brothers and sisters, where sisters pray for their brothers’ long life and well-being, and brothers give gifts in return.
