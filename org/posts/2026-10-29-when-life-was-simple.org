#+TITLE: When Life Was Simple
#+DESCRIPTION: At every stage of life, we think life is complicated. Years later, we look back at that same period and wonder why we thought it was so difficult.
#+DATE: 2026-10-29
#+CATEGORIES: Personal Essays
#+TAGS: family, parenting, relationships, children, life, parents
#+COMMENTS: true

When you are living through a particular time in your life, you don’t realize that it might be the best time of your life. Whether it is the time spent with your parents, your school years, college days, your first job when you earned just enough to support yourself, your early married life, or the time spent raising your first child and then your second. Every stage has something special about it, but we rarely recognize it while we are living through it.

Childhood is such an innocent stage of life. As a child, you worry about unit tests, first-term exams, mid-year exams, and finally the dreaded final exams. You get tired of studying, scared of failing, and frustrated with the constant expectations of parents and teachers. You are so occupied with these worries that you don’t realize you are living through a time that will never come back.

You have very few responsibilities. Your parents are there to take care of you, provide for you, and worry about things you don’t even know exist. Yet all you want is to grow up. You don’t want to listen to your parents or teachers. You want to make your own decisions. Your friends are your world, and spending time with them seems more important than anything else.

You think growing up will make life easier. You don’t yet understand that the freedom you are looking forward to comes with responsibilities of its own.

Then you enter college. For the first time, you don’t have to wear a uniform. No one is forcing you to attend classes. You can make your own choices and experience your first real taste of freedom. But soon, you find yourself worrying about grades, getting a job after college, and what the future holds for you. The freedom you had been waiting for comes with its own uncertainties.

Then you get your first job, perhaps after struggling for several months or perhaps soon after graduating. You finally earn enough to take care of yourself. Financial independence gives you the freedom you always wanted. You can spend your money as you wish, buy things without asking your parents, and make decisions on your own. Life seems good, but soon you want to earn more, find a better job, and eventually start a family.

Then you get married. You part ways with some of your freedom to gain companionship. Life is no longer just about you. There is another person whose happiness, dreams, and expectations become part of your life. You learn to make decisions together, adjust to each other’s habits, and sometimes put your own wishes aside. There are disagreements, adjustments, and new responsibilities, but there is also the happiness of having someone to share your everyday life with.

Those early years of marriage have their own charm. You are building a life together, figuring things out as you go, and making plans for the future. You may not have everything you want, but you have time for each other. You can go out whenever you wish, travel without much planning, or simply spend an evening doing nothing. Yet even during those years, you are thinking about what comes next. A better home, greater financial security, and perhaps starting a family.

Then your first child arrives, and life changes in ways you could never have imagined. You want to spend your whole life taking care of that tiny baby. You want to protect your child from everything and teach them all that you know. The baby brings immense joy, but also responsibilities you have never experienced before. As a new parent, you don’t always know what you are doing. You are constantly worried that you might accidentally hurt your child or do something wrong. Every little cry makes you anxious, and you wonder whether you are taking good enough care of this little person who depends entirely on you.

Suddenly, your days and nights revolve around feeding, changing diapers, putting the baby to sleep, and trying to get some sleep yourself. You are exhausted, but your responsibilities don’t end there. You now have a family of three to provide for. You work harder, think more seriously about your career, and worry about securing your child’s future. You want to spend every possible moment with your baby, but you also have to spend time away at work to provide for them. And sometimes, in the middle of all this exhaustion, you wish your child would grow up a little faster so that things might become easier.

And just when you think you have figured out parenting, your second child arrives. You are more confident this time. You know how to hold a baby, change diapers, and handle those sleepless nights. But now you have two little ones who need your love, time, and attention. Your older child is growing up, learning new things, and becoming more independent, while the younger one is just beginning the same journey. Your days become even busier. There is always something to do, someone to feed, someone to comfort, or someone asking for your attention. You sometimes wonder when life will become a little less demanding.

But time passes, and before you know it, the tiny babies who once slept in your arms are walking, talking, going to school, making friends, and developing personalities of their own. They still need you, perhaps just as much as before, but in different ways. Instead of worrying about feeding them or putting them to sleep, you worry about their education, friendships, happiness, and future. You want to guide them, help them make good decisions, and protect them from difficulties, while also giving them the freedom to grow.

And somewhere in the middle of all this, you begin to miss the days when they were little. The days when you could carry them in your arms, when their problems seemed easier to solve, and when they wanted to spend every waking moment with you. The very years you once wished would pass a little faster are now the years you wish you could experience again.

But then you realize that you are doing the same thing all over again. You are so occupied with work, responsibilities, your children’s education, and worries about the future that you sometimes forget to enjoy the present. Your children are still growing up. They still need you, and there are so many moments left to share with them. Your parents are growing older, too, and the time you get to spend with them becomes even more precious.

Perhaps ten or twenty years from now, you will look back at today with the same nostalgia. You will remember the busy mornings, the conversations with your children, the family dinners, and even the little disagreements that seem so important right now. You might wonder why you were always so worried when there was so much to be happy about.

Maybe life was never as simple as we remember it. Every stage had its own difficulties, and every stage gave us something we didn’t fully appreciate at the time.

Yet there is one time I know I will always miss: my childhood, spent with my brother and our parents. We were carefree and sometimes careless, unaware of how much our parents did to make our lives comfortable. Back then, I couldn’t wait to grow up. I never imagined that one day I would wish I could experience those ordinary days all over again.

And now, as I watch my own children grow, I wonder if they will someday feel the same way about their childhood. Perhaps ten or twenty years from now, I will also look back at the life I am living today, with all its worries, responsibilities, and moments of happiness, and find myself thinking how simple life was back then.
